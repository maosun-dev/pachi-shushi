# Single Impulse

One mark, one moment. The whole composition is the instant a fingertip meets glass:
a single numeral standing upright, and the disturbance it leaves behind spreading
outward in perfectly concentric, evenly decaying rings. Nothing else is allowed
on the field. Every element must earn its place by reading clearly at sixty pixels
on a crowded home screen and still rewarding a close look at full size.

Space is dark and deep, a near-black with the faintest cool green cast, so the mark
appears lit from within rather than printed on top. The rings are not decoration but
measurement: equal spacing, stroke weights that thin as they travel, opacity that
falls away on a precise curve, the kind of calibration that only comes from
painstaking, repeated adjustment by someone at the top of their craft.

Color is restricted to two voices. Ink-white for the numeral, carrying the weight
of a high-contrast serif cut with editorial confidence. A single living green,
the color of a positive balance, for the impulse and its echoes. No gradients that
call attention to themselves, no gloss, no bevels: depth comes only from tone and
from the quiet glow where the impulse begins.

Scale is the drama. The numeral is monumental, filling the field almost to its
edges, while the point of contact is small and exact. Their relationship is the
idea: a tiny action, an outsized result. Balance is optical, not mathematical;
the figure is nudged until it sits as a master letter-cutter would set it.

The finished work must look meticulously crafted: edges rendered at many times the
final size and reduced with care, curves without a single stray pixel, margins
that breathe. Master-level execution, nothing accidental, nothing extra.
